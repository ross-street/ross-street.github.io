StreetFest Speech

 

         What an incredible birthday present! Thank you all for coming to this conference dinner.

 

         Australia has been inhabited for more than sixty thousand years. I would like to acknowledge the Wattumattagal people as the traditional owners of the Macquarie University site. Undoubtedly they also had meaningful meetings here with people from other clans.

 

         When I was a child, this site was Italian market gardens. My Father was Senior Furniture Buyer and Inspector for the State Government. He watched the construction of Macquarie University as he supplied some of its furniture. He would come home and talk about it and I had a precognition that I would lecture here. My first visits to Macquarie University were to the German Department during 1968 to have my PhD thesis typed. 

 

         Submitting my thesis in August 1968, I took my first aeroplane flight to the University of Illinois. No direct flights then! Sydney, Fiji, Honolulu (where I heard that Russia had sent tanks into Czechoslovakia), San Francisco, Denver, Chicago, St Louis, Springfield . . . a couple of other stops on Ozark Airlines . . . and finally Champaign–Urbana! This was a new world: a university town in the cornfields; 150 mathematicians on the faculty; 450 mathematics graduate students; and a strange culture that American History and Hollywood movies had not prepared me for: forks in the wrong hand, French fries, and "you're welcome".

 

         When I accepted that postdoctoral fellowship I had thought Michael Barr and Alex Heller would be there: but by the time I arrived they were in McGill and CUNY. However John Gray remained and turned out to have a big influence on my research. Ehresmann, Eilenberg and Kelly had defined 2-categories but Gray was actually developing the theory. He insisted that it almost wrote itself as he, motivated by Lawvere's work, headed for a higher-dimensional comprehension scheme. K.T. Chen was also there; he is famous now for the iterated path integrals mentioned on Tuesday by Kapranov.

 

         A meeting of the Mid-West Category Seminar was planned for the University of Illinois in November 1968. I was scheduled to speak on my thesis. A phone call came from John Gray a night or two before the Seminar: would I like to come over to his house after dinner and meet a few people? A few people! These were the faces behind the names on the papers I had read. They were discussing Kan extensions — a concept I knew, but not under that name. Saunders Mac Lane, Myles Tierney, Jon Beck and John Kennison made a big impression on me at the Meeting. I was nervous about presenting my work to these experts.

 

         I was surprised many times by mathematicians in the US knowing a bit about my research and saying that I must be the person on whose work Max Kelly had been lecturing. Max's talks must have been better than mine at the Mid-West Category Meeting because, after my talk, Saunders invited me to walk with him around the campus. He put his hand on my shoulder and said: "Ross, you don't have to hit people with your best result right at the beginning. You need to warm up to it." In any case, he invited me up to Chicago to give a series of talks on triangulated categories.

 

         At Illinois I learnt analysis and worked on enriched categories, 2-categories and homotopy theory. Things haven't changed much!

 

         What I was also discovering was the wonderful warmth and friendliness of the international mathematical community: especially the category theorists. By the time I had spent the Category Summer of 1969 at Bowdoin College (Maine) and the Category Year 1969-70 at Tulane University (New Orleans) I had met most of the category theorists and many top mathematicians. At Bowdoin, Sammy Eilenberg and I became good friends. I didn't learn until much later when Sammy visited Macquarie that he and Mac Lane had been my external PhD thesis examiners. I am quite proud of that!

 

A lot happened in that year at Tulane. I met Margery (the love of my life) on Friday 13 September 1969. My Father died on 6 December and my Mother was not well. I came back to Australia for a month or so and Jack Duskin stepped in to take my Tulane lectures. I visited Macquarie University and Professor Fred Chong offered me a job to begin in mid 1970. A few months after returning to New Orleans, Margery and I were married after a three week engagement. Then we headed for Australia thinking we would eventually move back to the US.  Thirty-five years later we are still here!

 

Enough autobiography! In 1971, with Bob Walters at the University of Sydney, Max Kelly at the University of New South Wales, and me at Macquarie, we started what we now call the Australian Category Seminar. (I thought this was a reasonable name after Peter Johnstone referred to the "Australian School of Category Theory".)

 

Over the years, we have had our incredible high points, but also some disappointments and some great sorrows.  Scott Johnson, who died on the brink of completing his PhD, remains in our thoughts.

 

I would like to end by giving a lot of thanks to a lot of people. First, let me thank all our visitors over the years for enriching our seminars with their beautiful research. In the past this has been in small doses: one or two visitors at a time. Our Seminar is traditionally on Wednesday afternoons. This Conference is the complement of our Seminar with many visitors. Wednesday afternoon was the free time: although Thorsten Palm was under the impression that no talks were scheduled in order that we could have the Seminar as usual!

 

I am overwhelmed that clearly the time was ripe for a major conference in Sydney on our kind of mathematics. I thank you all for taking the long flight to be here. Now those of you who haven't been here before know what we of this ancient continent experience in visiting the newer world! Thanks especially to those who are giving talks.

 

Last September, a little group approached me in Melbourne at the annual meeting of the Australian Mathematical Society. Would I agree to have a conference in honour of my 60th birthday? Somewhat embarrassed, I did agree, thinking about 20 people might come.

 

       But you see the result. The organizers have done magnificently in inviting people and finding funds. Thank you Michael Batanin, William Chen, Alexei Davydov, Michael Johnson, Steve Lack, and Amnon Neeman. I don't think Michael Batanin knew what he was getting into: he has done a tremendous job. They have worked so smoothly that I do not even know all the contributions people have made. However, two people do stand out: Victoria Benning, our wonderful Department Administrator; and Daniel Steffen, our Web designer and electronic wiz. Thank you!

 

       And in Canberra, thanks to Chris Wetherell for taking care of accommodation.

 

       A generous donation from Steve and Rosemarie Johnson warranted our formalizing as a Macquarie University Centre (CoACT). I am profoundly grateful to them for allowing us to set up Fellowships and Scholarships in memory of Scott.

 

       Recent donations by Wesley Phoa and Margaret Morgan have given CoACT even more financial flexibility: a huge thank you to them.

 

       I also thank colleagues who would like to have been here but couldn't make it. I shall mention only my old friend Jack Duskin whom I hope we will be seeing again at conferences in the near future.

 

       Enjoy the rest of this conference, which is a tribute to all the members of CoACT.  I wish you safe journeys and happy times.
